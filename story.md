# Story notes: The Radio Man (የሬዲዮ ሰው)

Narrated by me (the child, now a young software developer), about my father. Amharic narration, English subtitles.

Beats:
1. A small Ethiopian town in the 1980s. Radios were how people heard the news, music and football.
2. My father, about 20, found a broken radio. Instead of throwing it away he opened it, just to see what was inside.
3. He had no teacher and no books. He traced wires by hand, night after night by a kerosene lamp, until the radio spoke again.
4. Word spread. Neighbours started bringing him their radios. His small table filled with wires, valves and screws.
5. On Saturdays people waited outside his door, so they would not miss the news or the match.
6. He told me once: "Everything that is broken can be understood. You just have to open it and look."
7. Today his hair is grey. He still keeps one old radio on the shelf.
8. I fix things too. Not radios: code. When a program breaks, I open it and look, the way he taught me.
9. Ending: his table became my laptop. Same curiosity, a new kind of wire.

Visual ideas: kerosene lamp, open radio back with coloured wires, hands with a small screwdriver, a line of neighbours, the old radio on a shelf next to a laptop. The narrator is never shown full-face (hands and laptop only).
