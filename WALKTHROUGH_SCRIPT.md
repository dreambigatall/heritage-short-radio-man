# Walkthrough script (≤ 5 minutes)

Record your screen with the Windows Game Bar (**Win+G**) or OBS. Talk in your own words; this is only a guide.

## 0:00–0:30: What it is
Show the 9:16 film for a few seconds. "This is The Radio Man, an 82-second Amharic film about my father. I didn't edit it by hand: it's generated by this pipeline from one file."

## 0:30–1:45: The architecture
Open `storyboard.json`, then `ARCHITECTURE.md`.
- "storyboard.json is the contract. It's validated against a JSON Schema before every run."
- "Five stages: narration with Gemini TTS, keyframes with FLUX.2 klein, shots, music with ACE-Step on Kaggle, and FFmpeg assembly."
- "Every artefact is named by a hash of its inputs, so a re-run only regenerates what changed."
- Open `runs.jsonl`: "Every model call is one line: model, seed, latency, cost."

## 1:45–2:45: One hard decision
Pick one: the TTS reading its instruction aloud plus the 10/day quota (F7, F8), or reference images locking the pose (E5). Show the FAILURES.md or EXPERIMENTS.md entry and say what you decided and why.

## 2:45–4:30: Live: change one shot, and only that shot regenerates
1. In PowerShell:
   ```powershell
   cd C:\Users\hp\Music\heritage-short
   (Get-Content runs.jsonl).Count
   ```
2. In `storyboard.json`, change **s05**'s `image_prompt`, for example add `, a cup of coffee on the corner of the table`. Save.
3. Run:
   ```powershell
   .venv\Scripts\python run.py film
   ```
4. Point at the output: every other shot says **`cached`**, and only `s05: generating ...` makes a call. *(You can pause the recording while it renders.)*
5. Show the evidence:
   ```powershell
   (Get-Content runs.jsonl).Count
   Get-Content runs.jsonl -Tail 1
   ```
   One new line, for `s05`.
6. Play the new s05 moment in the film.

## 4:30–5:00: Close
"What I learned: the model calls were the easy part. Quotas, overloads and language quirks were the real work, and the logs are what let me find and fix them."
